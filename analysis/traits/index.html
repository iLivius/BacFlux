    Trait profile¶

    The trait profile reads each genome for traits that matter in plant-growth promotion, biocontrol, stress tolerance and bioremediation: nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilisation, siderophores, plant hormones, osmoprotectants, the breakdown of aromatic compounds and the steps of denitrification. It serves the purpose BacFlux was developed for: the genomic characterisation of strains considered for biocontrol, biostimulant and biofertiliser applications. Each trait is called from a defined set of genes: for most traits several genes that must all be found, often side by side as in an operon; for a few, one marker gene.

    The layer is off by default; traits.run: true switches it on, in all modes. It writes to 04.annotation/traits/{sample}/, from rules in workflow/rules/shared/40_annotation.smk, and the summary across samples comes from workflow/rules/shared/90_report.smk. It adds jobs and a section to the MultiQC report, and changes no other stage's result.

    File What it is
    {sample}_trait_profile.tsv the result — one row per trait: called, partial or not called, with the genes behind it
    {sample}_trait_profile_audit.tsv why — one row per trait, with a fixed reason for its decision
    {sample}_trait_genes.tsv the evidence — one row per protein and gene: what matched, and whether it counted
    {sample}_trait_provenance.json the record — the fingerprints of the catalogue and of the three tables above, with the calling parameters
    09.report/traits_summary.tsv every sample's calls side by side, one column per trait

    Read them in this order: the summary, to see the batch at a glance; then a sample's profile; then its audit and gene table for any call you intend to quote.

    Terms used on this page
    3Di Foldseek's structural alphabet: one letter per residue that describes its 3D neighbourhood
    KO a KEGG Orthology number, KEGG's identifier for a gene family (K02588)
    EC an Enzyme Commission number, the identifier of an enzyme reaction (3.5.99.7)
    e-value how many hits at least this good the search expects by chance; smaller is better
    coverage the fraction of the protein (qcov) or of the catalogue structure (tcov) that the alignment spans

    How a gene is recognised¶

    Rules trait_db (or trait_db_local), trait_structure_query, trait_structure_search and trait_profile. Off by default; traits.run: true switches them on.
    In {sample}.faa and {sample}.tsv from the Bakta directory; EcoFoldDB; the ProstT5 model from Baktfold's database; {sample}.json from antiSMASH; Baktfold's {sample}.tsv when baktfold.run: true
    Out 04.annotation/traits/{sample}/
    Next 09.report/traits_summary.tsv, the MultiQC report and 10.workbooks/{sample}_traits.xlsx; user interpretation

    A gene is recognised in three ways, and one protein can carry all three.

    Structure (S). Every protein Bakta predicted, up to 4,000 amino acids, is turned into 3Di by the ProstT5 protein language model, on the CPU, without predicting a full structure. Foldseek then searches it against EcoFoldDB v2.0 (Ghaly et al. 2025) with -s 8, as EcoFoldDB's own method does. EcoFoldDB holds 205,334 predicted structures, 842 of which form its catalogue (EcoFoldDB_descriptions.txt): proteins of described function; the rest are other Swiss-Prot proteins. A hit is kept only when it passes the authors' filter:

    • the protein's best hit among all 205,334 structures (lowest e-value; the first listed wins a tie) is a catalogue entry;
    • its e-value is below 1e-10;
    • the alignment covers more than 0.8 of the protein and of the catalogue structure.

    The first condition keeps look-alikes out: an enzyme that shares a fold with a catalogue entry, but not its function, usually finds its best hit outside the catalogue. A kept hit gives S to every gene whose ecofolddb entries include that catalogue entry. A longer protein is not searched (not_searched_too_long), but its names are still checked.

    Bakta name (Q). A protein matches a gene by name when Bakta's gene symbol is exactly one of the gene's symbols, when one of its KO or EC numbers is listed for the gene, or when the gene's product pattern is found in Bakta's product. A symbol that unrelated genes share can be set to count only together with the product pattern or a KO (symbol_needs_product).

    Baktfold name (F). With baktfold.run: true, a protein that Bakta called "hypothetical protein" (a prefix such as (pseudo) aside) is tested the same way, with the name Baktfold gave it. Baktfold finds its names with the same ProstT5 model and Foldseek, so S+F is two structure searches agreeing, not a sequence-based name confirming a structure hit. See Baktfold.

    antiSMASH regions are supporting evidence only (How a trait is called).

    Borderline hits¶

    A kept structure hit is flagged borderline when it lies close to a cut-off or to a rival: its e-value is 1e-11 or above, within ten times the cut-off; its query or target coverage is 0.85 or less; or the best hit to another catalogue gene, or to a structure outside the catalogue, has an e-value at most ten times the best one. The flag never changes a decision. It marks a hit that a small change in the input, such as another assembly of the same strain, could push across a cut-off or hand to its rival.

    When a gene counts¶

    A protein counts for a gene when a name matches (Q or F), or when its kept structure hit (S) points to a gene whose s_alone is yes. Four points qualify this; the gene table gives each protein's reason.

    1. A veto wins. A gene can list products that rule it out (product_exclude): relatives acting on another substrate, unrelated genes with the same symbol, and regulators, receptors or binding proteins named after a pathway. When the pattern matches Bakta's or Baktfold's product, the protein does not count for that gene, whatever else matched, structure included.
    2. Most genes need a name. For most genes s_alone is no, so a structure hit counts only when a name agrees (reason structure_only_needs_name). Often the reason is a fold shared by enzymes of other pathways, as for ring-hydroxylating (Rieske) oxygenases, non-ribosomal peptide synthetases and HAD-family phosphatases; each gene's notes give its reason. A pathway name alone, such as "alcaligin biosynthesis protein", does not count for a required gene: a cluster's regulators and transporters carry it too.
    3. A pseudogene never counts. Bakta writes (pseudo) before the product of a gene it found broken, by a frameshift, an internal stop codon or a lost start codon. Where its evidence would count, its row reads pseudogene, and a set that lacks only such genes is partial.

    4. One protein, one gene per rule. A protein counts for two genes of one rule only when one source names both, as a fused PqqC/D, DfoC or UreAB does; Bakta's product, symbol, KO and EC count as one source. A fusion named after one part only shows the other as missing. Otherwise the protein counts once, for the first gene that applies:

      • the gene of its structure hit, when a structure hit counts alone for that gene (s_alone is yes);
      • the gene of Bakta's name;
      • the gene of Baktfold's name, last: Baktfold names a protein from its predicted structure, while Bakta's name comes from sequence similarity, which usually tells related enzymes apart.

      A protein whose structure hit is PcaG, the alpha chain of protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase, but which Bakta names as the beta chain, counts for pcaG only: one protein cannot fill both places of the pair. The other gene's row reads counted_for_other_gene, and the protein counts for it in no rule. A pseudogene is ranked the same way, but both of its rows keep the reason pseudogene, and their detail names the gene the broken protein is taken as.

    Bakta also marks a gene that runs off the end of its sequence: (5' truncated), (3' truncated) or (partial). BacFlux runs Bakta without --partial, so on a linear contig Bakta keeps only genes whose start and stop codons both lie inside it. These marks therefore appear only on the circular replicons of the nanopore and hybrid modes, most often on the replication gene, such as dnaA, that dnaapler put at the start. A circular replicon has no ends, so such a gene counts as usual, with a note in detail. Names are matched without these prefixes or (pseudo), in Bakta's product and in Baktfold's.

    How a trait is called¶

    Each rule names one or more alternative sets of required genes: + joins the genes of one set and | separates the alternatives, as in ureA+ureC | ureB+ureC. Sets are tried in the order listed: the first set that meets the called condition gives the call; failing that, the first one that qualifies as partial, and is not withheld (key genes, below), is reported.

    Decision When
    called every gene of the set counts, and the genes that must lie together do
    partial the set is complete but its genes are not together where the rule asks; or it is complete except for pseudogenes, which holds for a one-gene set too; or, with two or more genes, at least half of them count (rounded up)
    not called everything else, including a partial withheld by key_genes

    Co-localisation. A rule can ask that genes lie together: all means the whole set, otherwise groups separated by ;, such as nahAa+nahAb+nahAc+nahAd; nahB+nahC+nahD+nahE. A group holds when its genes form a chain on one contig, each at most traits.max_gene_gap positions from the previous one (default 10: up to nine other genes between). Positions count protein-coding genes in Bakta's order. A group applies only to a set that holds all its genes, so one rule can hold routes whose genes are arranged differently. Every contig is treated as linear: a chain across the start of a circular contig is not joined.

    Key genes. A rule can name key_genes: the genes that set its pathway apart from look-alikes, such as desA for desferrioxamine, since an aerobactin cluster also matches desB, desC and desD but has no desA. A set that lacks only pseudogenes is always reported. Any other partial is reported only when a key gene is in the genome, counted or as a pseudogene; otherwise it is withheld, and if no other partial stands, the trait is not called with the reason partial_withheld. The idea comes from MetaCyc's key reactions, which tell related pathway variants apart (Karp et al. 2011).

    Supporting genes are reported when they count, and no set requires them. Like any gene, a supporting gene can keep its protein from counting for a required gene of the same rule (When a gene counts, point 4). An entry antiSMASH:<type>, such as antiSMASH:NRP-metallophore, is reported when an antiSMASH region of exactly that type overlaps, on the same contig, a protein counted for a required gene.

    The two tables¶

    The rules live in two tables beside the script that applies them, each headed by a one-line # comment that says what it is (skip it when you load a table):

    • workflow/scripts/40_annotation/trait_rules_v1.tsv: 49 rules, one per trait, in the order of the seven groups below.
    • workflow/scripts/40_annotation/trait_genes_v1.tsv: the gene dictionary, 274 entries, one for each gene the rules name, saying how it is recognised.

    Each rule follows the primary papers in its reference column: the smallest set of genes that defines the pathway, its alternative routes, and which genes lie together. Each gene entry takes its structure evidence from EcoFoldDB's curated catalogue, its KO and EC numbers from KEGG and ExPASy ENZYME only where they belong to that gene alone, and its names from UniProt and RefSeq, written to match how Bakta names the protein. Vetoes cover known look-alikes, and s_alone is yes only where the fold belongs to that function alone; the notes give the reason for each entry.

    Editing either table makes the calls again, with the summary and the MultiQC report, but does not repeat the 3Di step (Cost and databases). A fresh clone of BacFlux does the same in an output folder made before, because it gives the tables new file dates. Before calling anything, the script checks both tables: the columns, unique trait_ids, exactly one entry for every gene a rule names and no unused entry, patterns that compile, yes/no values, and EcoFoldDB entries present in the catalogue. A failed check stops the job with a message naming the row.

    The rules table, trait_rules_v1.tsv:

    Column Contents
    trait_id, trait the identifier every output uses, and the trait in words
    application one or more of biofertiliser, biocontrol, biostimulant, stress tolerance and bioremediation, separated by commas; context marks a call that describes the strain's metabolism rather than supporting a product claim (What a call does not mean)
    required the alternative gene sets
    supporting genes and antiSMASH region types that are reported but not required, separated by |
    colocalised empty, all, or groups separated by ;
    key_genes optional: the genes that set the pathway apart from look-alikes, separated by | (Key genes)
    caveat what a call does not show; copied into the profile
    assay the laboratory test that would confirm the trait
    reference the primary papers behind the rule

    The gene dictionary, trait_genes_v1.tsv:

    Column Contents
    gene, protein the name the rules use, and the protein in words
    ecofolddb the catalogue entries that give structure evidence, named by the catalogue's Gene and Pathway/Activity joined by @; one name covers every entry with that Gene and Pathway/Activity, such as nifH@N2 fixation, which covers three structures; empty for a gene recognised by name only
    ko, ec the KO and EC numbers that count as a name match
    bakta_gene the gene symbols that count, matched exactly
    symbol_needs_product yes: a symbol counts only together with the product pattern or a KO
    product_include a pattern searched for in the product, ignoring case
    product_exclude a pattern that vetoes the protein for this gene
    s_alone yes: a structure hit counts without a name
    notes the biology behind the entry

    Lists inside a cell are separated by ;, and the patterns are Python regular expressions.

    Reading the output¶

    NA marks a missing value in all three tables.

    {sample}_trait_profile.tsv¶

    One row per rule, in table order.

    Column Contents
    sample, trait_id, trait, application which genome, which trait
    decision called, partial or not called
    genes the counted genes of the called or partial set, each as gene:locus_tag(evidence), with several proteins of one gene separated by commas and genes by semicolons; NA for a trait that is not called, and for a partial in which no gene counts
    missing for a partial, the genes of that set that did not count, separated by a comma and a space: each by its name, or, when only broken copies were found, as gene:locus_tag(pseudogene), several copies joined by a comma alone, as in nifD:SAMPLE1_01234(pseudogene),SAMPLE1_01240(pseudogene), nifE. A gene whose only broken copy went to another gene (When a gene counts, point 4) is written by its bare name. NA otherwise
    supporting the supporting genes that counted, and the antiSMASH region types found with their position
    borderline yes when a protein counted for the call carries a borderline structure hit for that gene, no when none does; NA for a trait that is not called
    structure_only the genes of the call counted from structure alone, with no name agreeing; NA when there are none, and for a trait that is not called
    caveat what the call does not show, from the rules table

    {sample}_trait_profile_audit.tsv¶

    One row per rule, with the columns sample, trait_id, decision, reason and detail. reason is a fixed word you can filter on; detail names the set and the genes behind it. For a partial, and for a trait that is not called, detail also lists the required genes of the rule's other sets that were found only as pseudogenes. A gene whose broken protein went to another gene (When a gene counts, point 4) is treated as not found, unless another protein counts for it or another broken protein is taken as it, and is written by its name; its row in the gene table shows the broken protein.

    cut -f3,4 {sample}_trait_profile_audit.tsv | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
    reason decision Means
    set_complete called one alternative set is complete, and its co-localisation holds
    set_complete_not_colocalised partial a set is complete, but its genes do not lie together where the rule asks
    set_complete_but_pseudogene partial each gene of a set counts or is found as a pseudogene, and at least one only as a pseudogene; a one-gene set too. detail names each such gene with its locus tag
    half_of_set_found partial at least half of a set of two or more genes counts
    partial_withheld not called a set qualifies as partial (other than one lacking only pseudogenes), but no key gene is in the genome, counted or as a pseudogene. detail reads no key gene (desA) found (...), with the withheld partial in brackets
    too_few_genes not called some genes count, but fewer than half of the closest set; detail says which are missing
    no_gene_found not called no required gene counts; detail names any required gene found only as a pseudogene

    {sample}_trait_genes.tsv¶

    One row per protein and gene with any evidence, sorted by contig and position: the place to check a call.

    Column Contents
    sample, locus_tag, contig, start, stop, strand the protein, from Bakta
    cds_index its position in the contig's gene order, the number co-localisation is measured in
    gene, traits the gene, and the trait_id of each rule that uses it
    counted yes or no
    evidence S, Q and F joined by +: structure, Bakta name, Baktfold name
    bakta_gene, bakta_product, ko, ec Bakta's annotation of the protein
    baktfold_product Baktfold's name for a protein Bakta called hypothetical
    ecofolddb_target, ecofolddb_gene, ecofolddb_pathway the best structure hit and its catalogue entry
    evalue, qcov, tcov that hit's e-value, as Foldseek printed it, and its two coverages
    borderline on a row whose evidence includes S: why that hit is borderline, or no; NA on other rows
    decision, reason, detail counted, not counted or not searched; a fixed reason; free text
    reason Means
    name_and_structure_agree a name match and a kept structure hit to one of the gene's ecofolddb entries; counted
    name_only a name match and no structure evidence for this gene, such as no hit, a failed hit or a kept hit to another entry (detail says which); counted
    structure_counts_alone a kept structure hit and no name, for a gene whose s_alone is yes; counted
    pseudogene evidence that would count by one of the three reasons above, on a protein Bakta marks as a pseudogene; not counted. When a different source names the protein for another gene of the same rule, detail says which of the two it is taken as (When a gene counts, point 4)
    counted_for_other_gene evidence that would count, on a protein that counts instead for another gene of the same rule (When a gene counts, point 4); not counted, and detail names both genes and why
    structure_only_needs_name a kept structure hit and no name, for a gene whose s_alone is no; not counted
    vetoed_by_name the product matched the gene's product_exclude; not counted by any evidence
    catalogue_gene_unused a kept structure hit to a catalogue entry that no gene of the gene dictionary uses; listed without a gene
    not_searched_too_long a protein longer than 4,000 amino acids, not searched by structure; listed without a gene, and its names are still checked. detail gives its length

    A protein that matched by name but whose best structure hit is a catalogue entry that failed the filter keeps its name row: the failed hit fills the structure columns, and detail says why it failed.

    09.report/traits_summary.tsv¶

    One row per sample and one column per trait_id, in table order, each cell called, partial or not called.

    The MultiQC report shows the same calls in its Trait profile section, after Bakta, from the plot files in 09.report/traits_summary_mqc/:

    • Call counts: for each strain, how many traits are called, partial and not called. The counts help to find strains in a batch; they do not rank strains by benefit.
    • One heatmap per application label, strains in rows and traits in columns. Each trait appears once, under its exact label, so biofertiliser, biocontrol has its own panel. The axis shows each trait's trait_id, and the panel lists every ID with the trait's name.
    • References and assays: for each trait, the papers behind its rule, a suggested laboratory assay and its caveat, from the catalogue.

    Dark blue is called, light blue partial and grey not called. The heatmaps draw them from the numbers 2, 1 and 0, which stand for the three categories, not for a score of confidence or activity. A separate References section of the report lists the papers of the tools and of the trait methods.

    {sample}_trait_provenance.json¶

    Written last by trait_profile.py, after the three tables. It records the SHA-256 fingerprint (a code computed from every byte of a file) of the two catalogue tables, of EcoFoldDB's descriptions and of the three tables, each beside its file name, with the calling parameters. The MultiQC report and the traits workbook quote the catalogue's caveats, assays and references beside the calls, so rule traits_summary and rule traits_workbook compare these fingerprints with the files they read first: a catalogue edited after the calls were made, or an edited table, stops them, with rule trait_profile to be run again. Only contents are compared, so an output folder that has been moved still passes.

    What a call does not mean¶

    A call is genomic evidence, not a phenotype

    A call says that the genes for a trait are present. It does not show that they are expressed, that the proteins are active, or that the strain helps a plant. The caveat column says what each call leaves open, and the assay column of the rules table names the test that would confirm it.

    not called is not the same as absent. A gene can be missed: its protein may carry no name the patterns recognise while the gene needs a name to count, the protein may be too long to be searched by structure, Bakta's gene finder may not predict a short gene at all, or the assembly may break the gene. A set that the rule wants together, split across contigs of a draft assembly, is reported as partial with the reason set_complete_not_colocalised, not as called. Read the audit before reporting a trait as missing.

    partial is a lead, not half a trait. Check missing in the profile, and the gene table for the proteins behind it.

    A call can rest on a single gene. Some rules accept one marker gene as a set, such as acdS for ACC deaminase or nosZ for nitrous oxide reduction. When that gene is listed in structure_only, the call rests on a structure hit alone, with no name confirming it; only a gene whose s_alone is yes, such as nosZ, can count this way, so acdS always needs a name.

    A call says nothing about safety. The genes behind a plant-beneficial trait are not confined to harmless bacteria: pyoverdine, a siderophore of the catalogue, is also a virulence factor of Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Meyer et al. 1996). Whether a strain can be used rests on its taxonomy and risk group, and on its resistance and virulence genes and their mobility (AMR workbook), not on how many traits it carries. The trait profile ranks nothing.

    context describes the strain, not a product claim. The application column gives each trait the use it points to. context marks a trait whose call describes the strain's metabolism but does not, on its own, support a biofertiliser, biocontrol, biostimulant or bioremediation claim. Three kinds of traits carry it:

    • the four denitrification steps, nitrate_reduction to n2o_reduction, context only: nitrate reduction is the first step both of denitrification, which loses nitrogen from the soil, and of nitrate ammonification, which keeps it as ammonium;
    • siderophore_mycobactin, context only: mycobactin is the siderophore of mycobacteria and their relatives, so a call says which genus you are holding rather than that the strain supplies a plant with iron;
    • the central aromatic routes, catechol and protocatechuate ring cleavage and phenylacetate degradation, bioremediation, context: many aromatic pollutants end up in them, but they are also the ordinary exit for plant phenolics in many soil bacteria. A pollutant claim rests on an upstream route, such as naphthalene_upper_pathway or toluene_monooxygenase_aerobic.

    The label never changes a decision.

    borderline: yes marks a call in which a counted protein carries a structure hit close to a cut-off or to a rival. Check that hit in the gene table before quoting the call.

    Cost and databases¶

    The 3Di step dominates: about 1 h 50 min per genome on 16 CPU cores, and it grows with the number and length of the proteins. It turns every protein into a 3Di sequence, not only the hypothetical ones Baktfold looks at, so the trait profile and Baktfold do not share this step. The Foldseek search then takes about 2 minutes per genome, and the calls seconds.

    The layer needs two databases:

    • Baktfold's database, for the ProstT5 model inside it, even when baktfold.run is false: about 22 GB, from directories.baktfold_db, or downloaded whole by baktfold install on the first run.
    • EcoFoldDB v2.0: a 0.57 GB archive from its Zenodo record, checked against links.ecofolddb_md5 and unpacked to about 0.8 GB in 04.annotation/traits/databases/, or a copy you already hold, from directories.ecofolddb_db.

    Use the Zenodo copy of EcoFoldDB

    The copy of EcoFoldDB in the authors' GitHub repository names some catalogue entries differently from the Zenodo record, and the gene dictionary needs the Zenodo names. A copy given in directories.ecofolddb_db is checked before any job starts: when an entry the gene dictionary uses is missing from its EcoFoldDB_descriptions.txt, the run stops and names it.

    The structure files in 04.annotation/traits/{sample}/structure/ are kept, so the 3Di step runs once per genome. The Foldseek hit table, about 170 MB per genome, is temporary: making the calls again, after a table edit for example, repeats only the 2-minute search. Fetching EcoFoldDB again repeats the search and the calls; fetching or linking Baktfold's database again repeats the 3Di step too. The archive clean-up (workflow/scripts/clean_workdir.sh) removes the structure files and both databases, and a later run in a cleaned folder repeats the 3Di step. Switching baktfold.run on in a folder that already holds trait profiles makes the calls again, with Baktfold's names.

    Resources¶

    Rule Threads Memory
    trait_structure_query capped at 24 16 GB requested from a cluster scheduler, or ram_gb if lower
    trait_structure_search capped at 24 none declared
    trait_profile 1 none declared
    traits_summary 1 none declared

    "Capped at 24" has the meaning given under Annotation. The 3Di step runs ProstT5 on the CPU; like Baktfold's own rule, it is held to its threads and asks no internet service for the model, so it runs on a compute node without internet. trait_db is a local rule: on a cluster it runs on the computer that runs Snakemake, which is the one with internet. It takes wget, tar and the gzip that tar calls from workflow/envs/download.yaml; md5sum, awk, mkdir, basename, rm and ls come from the computer, as part of every Linux base system. The 3Di and search rules run in workflow/envs/baktfold.yaml, which carries ProstT5 and Foldseek, and the two scripts in workflow/envs/python.yaml.

    What to check afterwards¶

    • 09.report/traits_summary.tsv, or the trait heatmaps in 09.report/multiqc_report.html: every sample at a glance.
    • {sample}_trait_profile_audit.tsv: the reason behind each decision.
    • 10.workbooks/{sample}_traits.xlsx: the calls with their genes, caveats, suggested assays and references, and the plasmid and phage context of every gene, in one file per strain (Per-strain workbooks).
    • logs/trait_db.log: the download, and whether the MD5 matched. Nothing is extracted when it did not. With directories.ecofolddb_db set, logs/trait_db_local.log takes its place.
    • logs/trait_structure_query_{sample}.log, logs/trait_structure_search_{sample}.log and logs/trait_profile_{sample}.log: one per sample per step; logs/traits_summary.log for the summary. A WARNING in the trait_profile log that the hit table holds no hits means that every trait of that sample is decided from names alone, with no structure evidence.
