    ONT adapter trimming and the adapter screen¶

    An Oxford Nanopore (ONT) read starts with the sequencing adapter that brought the DNA molecule to the pore, and in a barcoded library the barcode and its flanks follow it. Left in the reads, both can end up in the assembly. BacFlux therefore removes them from the ONT reads before assembly, in nanopore and hybrid mode, and searches every finished genome for adapter sequence afterwards, in all four modes.

    Practical guidance

    • The default, ont_adapter: detect, works out from the reads whether anything is left to trim. Reads that are already trimmed pass through unchanged, so leaving the default on costs a few minutes and nothing else.
    • Set ont_adapter: off to keep an assembly made by an earlier BacFlux unchanged: with trimming on, a long-read output folder made before v2.2.0 assembles again (see Upgrading an output folder).
    • Read the Adapters table of the MultiQC report: it says what was trimmed from the reads, which barcodes they carried, and whether the finished genome still holds adapter.
    • The screen covers the common adapters only. Before a GenBank submission, also run NCBI's own screen, FCS-adaptor, on the genome.

    Terms used on this page
    adapter the short synthetic DNA that brings a molecule to the nanopore; the kit 14 ligation, native and rapid adapters share the top strand CCTGTACTTCGTTCAGTTACGTATTGCT
    barcode block in a barcoded library, the barcode with its two flanks: for native barcoding AAGGTTAA, the 24 bp barcode and CAGCACCT (40 bp); for rapid barcoding GCTTGGGTGTTTAACC, the 24 bp barcode and a 50 bp constant sequence (90 bp)
    Mu end that 50 bp constant sequence: the right end of phage Mu, identical to the last 50 bp of the Mu genome (NC_000929.1)
    trimmed sequence what fastplong removes from the reads of one sample
    chimeric read one read that holds two molecules joined end to end, with an adapter between them
    end hit adapter sequence within 100 bp of the end of a linear contig
    bleed-through reads of one sample on a multiplexed flow cell that are assigned to another sample's barcode

    Why¶

    Dorado can remove adapters and barcodes when it basecalls, but reads made by older basecallers, or with other settings, can keep them, and a FASTQ file does not say which. In the reads, the adapter and barcode sit at the read ends. In the assembly, they end up wherever many reads end at the same place, which is at the ends of linear contigs. A circular contig has no ends, so a complete bacterial genome is rarely affected; an incomplete one, or a linear replicon, is.

    In the BacFlux hybrid assembly of Klebsiella pneumoniae TUM24772 made without trimming, the linear contig contig_2 (16,144 bp) ended in 112 bp of library construct: part of the rapid adapter, the rapid flank, rapid barcode RB11 and the Mu end. The ONT reads (SRR30870266) were made with the Rapid Barcoding Kit SQK-RBK004, and the construct sat at the start of most of them. It was already at the end of Flye's contig, and it was still there after dnaapler (which leaves a linear contig unrotated), Medaka and Polypolish. NCBI screens genome submissions for contamination, and FCS-adaptor, the adaptor screen NCBI provides for genomes before submission to GenBank, trims adaptor found within 100 bp of a sequence end (FCS-adaptor output): it would have flagged that contig end.

    Trimming the reads with the exact sequence also lets fastplong split chimeric reads at the adapter between their two molecules.

    Settings¶

    The key goes in the block of the mode, parameters.nanopore or parameters.hybrid:

    ont_adapter Effect
    detect (the default; also empty or missing) BacFlux works the sequence out from the reads, or copies reads that are already trimmed unchanged
    a sequence of 20 to 200 bases of A, C, G and T that sequence is trimmed, as written
    off (also an unquoted off, no or false) no trimming: Filtlong reads the raw reads

    An unquoted on, yes or true names none of these and stops the run. A sequence that is a short unit repeated end to end, such as a homopolymer or a microsatellite, is refused when the workflow starts, because trimming it would cut genome from the read ends. A sequence that lacks the adapter core, or is shorter than 50 bp, gets a NOTE. The adapter screen has no settings apart from where UniVec is downloaded from (links.univec_link and links.univec_sha256, see Configuration).

    How the sequence is found¶

    Rule find_ont_adapter reads every read of the sample once, in seconds to a minute for an isolate.

    1. The adapter. The first 150 bp of every read are searched for the 20 bp core of the adapter top strand, TTCGTTCAGTTACGTATTGC. The core must match exactly, so reads whose adapter carries a basecalling error there are not counted, and the share of reads with the adapter is a lower bound. Older kits use other adapters: the RBK004 rapid adapter ends in TTCGTTCAGTTACG and then the rapid flank, without the core's last six bases, so its reads are found by step 4 instead.
    2. The block after the adapter. In a barcoded library most reads continue after the adapter with the same barcode block; in a library without barcodes they continue into the genome, which differs from read to read. BacFlux takes the longest block, up to 120 bp, that at least 60 % of the reads with the adapter share.
    3. ONT's published barcodes. The sequence after the adapter is compared with the 96 native and 96 rapid barcodes ONT publishes in its Chemistry Technical Document; a read counts for a barcode when at most 3 of the 24 barcode bases differ. The list names the barcode and sorts the reads of a file that holds several barcodes. The sequence trimmed for each barcode is the block found in its own reads, unless that block is shorter than the published one: with about 1 % basecalling errors per base, fewer than 60 % of the reads carry a whole 90 bp rapid block without one, so the shared stretch ends inside the Mu end, and the published block is trimmed instead. A kit or barcode the list lacks is trimmed as found.
    4. Barcode blocks without the adapter core. A read without the core is searched for a whole barcode block in its first 150 bp, in two ways: a native or rapid block with exact flanks, and, for rapid barcodes, the start of the Mu end (GTTTTCGCATTTATCG) with a published rapid barcode in the 24 bp in front of it. The second search exists because the first misses whole runs: on R9.4.1 reads the 16 bases right after the adapter are often called wrong. In the TUM24772 reads, BLAST found the RB11 block at the start of 18,284 of the 19,091 reads, but its 16 bp flank was exact in 37 of them; RB11 followed by the start of the Mu end was exact in 3,180.

    5. The decision.

      • When fewer than 1 % of the reads start with the adapter, and fewer than 1 % with a barcode block, the reads count as already trimmed and pass through unchanged.
      • When published barcodes follow the adapter, each in at least 1 % of the reads with the adapter (and at least 10 reads), the adapter followed by each barcode's block is trimmed.
      • When the adapter is followed by no published barcode, the adapter and the common block after it are trimmed, or the adapter alone in a library without barcodes.
      • When the adapter core is missing but at least 1 % of all reads start with a barcode block (step 4), each such barcode is trimmed: for a published barcode its published block, which fastplong matches despite basecalling errors and removes together with whatever unrecognised adapter sits in front of it.

      Two or more barcodes that each hold at least 10 % of the barcoded reads get a NOTE, because such a file may hold several samples. A barcode below 10 % is usually bleed-through from a neighbouring sample on the same flow cell: it is trimmed when it reaches 1 % of the reads, without a note, and the second most common barcode is reported with its share either way. A block that is a short tandem repeat is never added. When the adapter is followed by several different blocks that are not published barcodes, only the part they share is trimmed, with a NOTE.

    The file 01.reads/{sample}/ont/adapters/{sample}_adapter_scan.tsv counts the reads with the adapter, and with a barcode block and no adapter core, in every block of 10,000 reads. A batch of already-trimmed reads in an otherwise untrimmed file shows up there.

    What fastplong does with it¶

    Rule trim_ont_adapters runs fastplong 0.7.1 with the most common sequence (-s) and a FASTA file of every sequence to trim, in both orientations (-a). fastplong searches the start of every read for the -s sequence and the end for its reverse complement; the -a sequences are searched at both ends as written, which is how every barcode of a file holding several is trimmed, and how a rapid block copied to the read end in its forward orientation is found. fastplong allows a quarter of the bases to differ and removes the sequence together with everything on the outer side of it. BacFlux adds:

    Option Why
    --trimming_extension 0 fastplong's default cuts 10 more bases past the sequence, which after a whole adapter and barcode are genome
    -Q -L fastplong's quality and length filters stay off: Filtlong, the next step, is the read filter of the workflow
    --disable_chimera_splitting, when a sequence to trim is shorter than 50 bp fastplong also cuts a read where it finds the sequence in the middle (a chimeric read), with the same tolerance as at the ends, so a short sequence matches genome by chance. On public reads of the fungus Botrytis cinerea B05.10 (SRR40741277), the 28 bp adapter alone split 1,041 reads, 1,001 of them in error, while the 68 bp adapter and barcode split 73, all genuine joins

    The two pieces of a split read are named chimera-segment-1-<read> and chimera-segment-2-<read>. Read headers are otherwise kept as they are, including the basecaller tags the Medaka model check reads. When there is nothing to trim, fastplong runs with -A and copies the reads byte for byte. fastplong's own guess of the adapter is never used: it looks only at the first reads of a file, which can be a batch of already-trimmed reads, and takes the most common sequence at the read ends, which can be genome.

    Rule check_ont_trimming then counts, among the reads Filtlong kept (the reads Flye assembles), those that still start with the adapter core and those that still start with the end of the trimmed sequence. More than 1 % for either prints a NOTE; the run goes on. The second count matters for older kits, whose adapter lacks the core.

    The adapter screen¶

    Rule blast_adapters searches the finished genome, contigs_final.fasta, of every sample in every mode, with BLAST (blastn -task blastn-short) for:

    Sequences Source
    ONT adapters, the 96 native barcodes and the 96 rapid barcodes with their flanks ONT Chemistry Technical Document (shipped with BacFlux)
    PacBio and MGI adapters NCBI UniVec (shipped with BacFlux)
    Illumina adapters and primers NCBI UniVec build 10.1, downloaded into the run, because Illumina's sequences are copyrighted
    the sequence trimmed from the sample's reads rule find_ont_adapter, when BacFlux trimmed the reads

    The genome as delivered is searched, not Flye's draft: it is the genome every later step annotates and the one a user submits, it exists in all four modes, and dnaapler rotates only circular contigs. A linear contig keeps Flye's ends through dnaapler and polishing.

    Rule screen_adapters keeps a hit when at least 90 % of its aligned bases are identical and it is at least 20 bp long within 100 bp of the end of a linear contig, or at least 28 bp long, a whole adapter, inside a contig. The end rule does not ask how much of an adapter record is covered, because adapter is often cut short at the very end of a contig. Overlapping hits form one locus. Two cases need care in bacteria:

    • Circular contigs have no ends. Their two ends are an arbitrary cut, so every hit on a circular contig is judged by the internal rule. To see an adapter lying across that cut, the search runs on a copy of each circular contig extended by its own first 200 bp. Topology comes from Flye, through the Bakta replicon table of the long-read modes; in illumina and contigs mode it is not known, and every contig is treated as linear.
    • The Mu end is genome in some bacteria. A genome that carries a Mu prophage holds the same 50 bp as the end of every rapid barcode. A locus made only of the Mu end is therefore listed but not counted when it lies inside a contig, where a rapid-kit remnant would bring its barcode along; at the end of a linear contig it counts.

    Each locus gets a counted (yes/no) and a reason column: adapter_at_contig_end, mu_end_at_contig_end, adapter_inside_contig or mu_end_inside_contig. The screen changes no contig.

    Before a GenBank submission

    The lists cover the common adapters only. NCBI provides its own adaptor screen, FCS-adaptor (FCS), distributed as a container, to clean genomes before submission to GenBank; run it on the genome before you submit.

    The files in 01.reads/{sample}/ont/adapters/ exist only for ONT samples that BacFlux trimmed.

    Public runs scanned¶

    Before trimming was added, the scan of step 1 to 5 was run on eight public bacterial ONT runs:

    Run Organism Kit Reads What the scan found Decision
    SRR30870266 K. pneumoniae TUM24772 SQK-RBK004, R9.4.1 19,091 RB11 in front of the Mu end in 19.9 % of the reads (step 4); no adapter core trim the 90 bp RB11 block
    ERR16717241 Bacillus cereus SQK-RBK114 70,147 RB10 without adapter core in 31.8 % of the reads, nearly all of them shorter than 1 kb; RB04 in 2.4 % of the barcoded reads trim the 90 bp RB10 block
    SRR41339580 K. pneumoniae SQK-NBD114-96, basecalled live in MinKNOW 25.09 56,128 adapter in 0.02 %, its barcode NB23 in 0.2 % nothing to trim
    SRR41339581 Escherichia coli as above, same flow cell 88,966 adapter in 0.05 %, NB22 in 0.35 % nothing to trim
    SRR41339582 Citrobacter freundii as above, same flow cell 54,032 adapter in 0.04 %, NB21 in 0.3 % nothing to trim
    SRR41610995 Pectobacterium punjabense SQK-NBD114 81,463 adapter in 0.87 % nothing to trim
    SRR41617180 Methylobacterium ajmalii SQK-LSK114 with EXP-NBD114, trimmed by its submitters 95,398 barcode block in 0.26 % nothing to trim
    SRR36766647 K. pneumoniae not stated 103,071 adapter in 0.31 % nothing to trim

    Six of the eight runs were already trimmed, the three runs basecalled live in MinKNOW among them. In those three runs of one flow cell, the few reads still starting with a barcode block carried mostly the sample's own barcode (NB23, NB22 and NB21: 120, 311 and 161 reads); no other barcode appeared in more than 5 reads. In the B. cereus run the rapid construct stayed mainly on short fragments: 62.7 % of the reads shorter than 1 kb start with it, against 0.1 to 0.2 % of the longer ones, and Filtlong drops reads shorter than 1 kb. The TUM24772 run carried it at the start of nearly every read, long and short alike (18,284 of 19,091 by BLAST). When the reads of TUM24772 and of B. cereus were put into one file, as a mislabelled file would be, the scan trimmed both barcodes and printed the several-barcodes NOTE.

    What it did on K. pneumoniae TUM24772¶

    The TUM24772 reads (SRR30870266) went through nanopore mode twice, with the same settings, once with ont_adapter: detect and once with off:

    off detect
    reads fastplong trimmed — 18,636 of 19,091, about 113 bp each: the RB11 block and the adapter in front of it
    chimeric reads split — 13, into 25 pieces
    reads Flye assembled that still start with the adapter core, or with the end of the block — 0, and 30 of 12,116 (0.25 %)
    Flye's contigs 6: the same five circles, and a linear contig of 15,344 bp 5, all circular: the chromosome and four plasmids
    contig ends with adapter 1: the end of the linear contig, 88 bp of the RB11 block 24 bp from the end none

    The linear contig of the untrimmed reads is a near-copy of part of the 23.8 kb plasmid (99.4 % identity over 11.6 kb), and it ends in the construct. With the reads trimmed, Flye assembles no such contig; its sequence lies inside the circular plasmid, whose coverage rises from 87× to 106×, about what the linear contig held (17×). The hybrid assembly of the same untrimmed reads had the same kind of contig, the 16,144 bp contig_2 described under Why. The off arm was run as far as Flye, and its contigs were searched with the same screen.

    Upgrading an output folder¶

    With trimming on, Filtlong reads a new file, the trimmed reads, so an output folder made before v2.2.0 runs Filtlong, Flye and every later step again for every long-read sample, even when the scan finds nothing to trim: the copy fastplong then makes is still a new file, and Snakemake compares file times, not contents. In hybrid mode the Illumina steps run again too, because their trimmed reads are temporary files that the later steps need. ont_adapter: off keeps Filtlong's earlier input, and with it the earlier assembly; the adapter screen then runs on it on its own, as it does in illumina and contigs mode, where nothing else changes.

    Limits¶

    • The barcode check sees only what is still in the reads. Reads demultiplexed and trimmed at basecalling carry nothing to count, so the check then says nothing about bleed-through or a mislabelled file.
    • Below 1 % of the reads, the adapter or a barcode counts as absent, and nothing is trimmed. A few untrimmed reads ending at a linear contig end could still leave adapter there; the screen reports it.
    • The screen searches for the adapters on its lists. An adapter of another platform or kit is not found unless it is on them, or unless BacFlux trimmed it from the reads.
